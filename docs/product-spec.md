# Product specification

## Purpose

Research Bot supports a human researcher rather than taking over authorship or research decisions. Sustainability is a useful starting subject, but project topics are unrestricted.

## First workflow

1. Create a project with a working title, rough thoughts, and an optional research question.
2. Ask the methods coach to identify missing scope and suggest an ordered plan.
3. Review the suggested question, method, feasibility, and milestones.
4. Ask the evidence finder for candidate sources and open the originals yourself.
5. Save relevant sources and your own reading notes in a literature matrix.
6. Ask the brainstorming partner for possible connections or alternative explanations.
7. Write your own notes or draft, then request grammar-only corrections.
8. Accept individual edits and export the project notes, source list, and plan.

## Four work areas

### Grammar

Show original text, proposed text, and an edit list. Correct only grammar, spelling, and punctuation. Do not replace words with synonyms, polish style, expand arguments, invent facts, or change certainty. If fixing a sentence requires interpreting ambiguous meaning, ask for clarification and leave that passage unchanged. Every edit requires human acceptance; rejection preserves the original.

### Evidence and literature review

Search for relevant academic articles, institutional reports, documents, and forums. For each result store its title, authors or issuing organization when available, date, canonical URL, DOI when available, source category, and retrieval date. Explain why it may be relevant and whether the agent inspected full text, an abstract, metadata, or a snippet.

Provide a literature matrix with research question, method, setting/sample, key findings, limitations, and the researcher's notes. Populate only fields supported by retrieved material and label missing fields. A metadata match or working URL does not establish that a paper supports a claim. Forum discussions provide perspectives and leads; they are not automatically scientific evidence. Record search queries and inclusion/exclusion decisions for reproducibility. Ask before generating a literature-review narrative; source discovery and organization are the default.

### Brainstorming

Offer candidate questions, possible explanations, counterarguments, gaps worth investigating, and practical feasibility questions. Distinguish supported observations from untested ideas. Invite the researcher to select or reject directions. Do not insert brainstormed material into their draft.

### Methods and planning

Teach methods in plain language with examples relevant to the project. Explain when qualitative interviews, surveys, experiments, case studies, secondary-data analysis, literature reviews, and sustainability-specific approaches such as life-cycle assessment may fit. Do not select a method solely from the topic name.

Turn rough thoughts into steps: narrow the problem; formulate the question; identify existing work; select and justify the method; define data and sampling; assess feasibility and ethics where applicable; collect evidence; analyze it; examine limitations; document and communicate results. Each step includes a purpose, expected output, dependency, and completion check. Timelines are estimates agreed with the researcher.

## First-version scope

- One researcher on one computer; local project storage.
- Multiple projects, editable notes, a source library, a literature matrix, and a plan.
- Four separately selectable agent roles coordinated by application code.
- Official ChatGPT sign-in, consent, sign-out, and account/usage status.
- On-demand runs, cancellation, errors, and Markdown/JSON export.
- No automatic rewriting or publication.

The researcher mentioned two possible approaches but described only the human-led workflow. The second approach remains unspecified and is outside this initial scope.

## Example: sustainability research

Rough thought: “I want to investigate food waste at my university.” The coach helps narrow the location, time period, access constraints, and measurable question. The evidence finder locates candidate papers and institutional guidance. The brainstorming partner suggests possible influences to investigate. The researcher chooses the question, gathers and evaluates evidence, and writes the conclusions.
